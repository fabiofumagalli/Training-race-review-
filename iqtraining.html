Athlete Type

Speed through the manoeuvre aligned on head/stern to wind, kn

Each thin line is one manoeuvre, from 10 s before to 20 s after the bow (tack) or stern (gybe) crosses the wind. Thick lines are each athlete’s average. The dashed green line is the foiling threshold.

How to read it

Entry: average speed from −10 to −4 s. Min: lowest speed during the manoeuvre. Kept: min ÷ entry. Recovery: seconds to get back to 90% of entry speed. Distance lost: over 20 s (−5 to +15 s), how much less ground was gained upwind (tack) or downwind (gybe) than by holding the entry VMG. Foiling: min speed stayed above the foiling threshold (set it in Speed).

Manoeuvres are found from the change of tack relative to the race wind (or the session wind): if that wind is badly off, the manoeuvre type will be too. Click a row to see it on the map. Only the selected time range is analysed.

Real polar speed by true wind angle

How to read it

Each dot is one second of sailing: distance from the centre is speed, angle is TWA (wind at the top, both tacks folded together). The line is the athlete’s good speed at that angle (90th percentile in 10° bins). A line further out at the same angle means faster; dots higher up when upwind mean pointing higher.

TWA uses the wind logged for the race, so the angles are only as accurate as the wind reading on the water.
